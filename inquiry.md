James’s inquiry begins with a practical distinction: the condition that becomes visible is not always the condition that originated the problem. When work crosses fields, evidence can remain locally valid while its dependencies, translations, and limits disappear from view.

This is why provenance matters. Provenance is more than citation. It includes the path by which a claim, method, category, or intervention arrived; the transformations it underwent; the boundaries it crossed; and the authority it is permitted to carry.

## Recursive inquiry

The practice is recursive because inquiry must be able to examine its own framing. A question may need to return to its definitions, source conditions, dependencies, or institutional assumptions before it can responsibly move forward.

That recursion is bounded. It is not an invitation to endless abstraction. The aim is to make enough of the structure visible that a cross-field encounter can proceed without pretending the participating fields have become one.

## Instruments and judgment

AI and other computational tools can assist with discovery, comparison, dependency mapping, and drafting. They remain instruments within accountable human judgment. Sources, boundaries, uncertainty, and authorship must stay visible.
